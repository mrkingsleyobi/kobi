<callout>We've spent decades optimizing for intelligence and technical skills. AI is about to make those much less valuable. What should we optimize for instead?</callout>

The economic landscape is shifting in ways we haven't seen since the Industrial Revolution. Intelligence — once the scarce resource that commanded high premiums — is becoming abundant.

This raises uncomfortable questions: If intelligence is cheap, what becomes valuable? What should we do with our lives? What's the relationship between money and meaning in a world of AI?

Here's how I'm thinking about it.

---

## The Great Decoupling

<tutorial>We're heading toward a decoupling of economic value from traditional intelligence-based skills. This creates both crisis and opportunity.</tutorial>

**What's becoming cheap:**

- Coding and technical implementation
- Data analysis and pattern recognition
- Content generation and basic creativity
- Routine cognitive work of all kinds
- Translation, summarization, research

**What's becoming expensive:**

- Taste and judgment
- Strategic thinking and synthesis
- Human connection and trust
- Novel ideas and genuine innovation
- Leadership and influence

<aside>The economic premium is shifting from intelligence to wisdom, from technical skill to human judgment.</aside>

---

## Redefining Human Value

<callout>We've tied human worth to economic productivity for too long. As AI takes over more productive work, we need new ways of thinking about human value.</callout>

**Beyond economic productivity:**

- **Creativity**: Not generating content, but having something genuine to express
- **Community**: Building and maintaining meaningful relationships
- **Wisdom**: Living well and helping others do the same
- **Experience**: Being present for the fullness of human experience
- **Service**: Contributing to something larger than yourself

**The question isn't**: What can I do that AI can't?
**The question is**: What gives my life meaning and value?

---

## Three Economic Models

<tutorial>Different paths through the AI transition will make sense for different people. There's no one right answer.</tutorial>

### **Model 1: AI Leverage**

Use AI to dramatically increase your productivity and earnings.

- **Who it's for**: People who want to maximize income and impact
- **How it works**: Become an expert at directing AI systems
- **Trade-off**: High stress, constant adaptation, potential burnout

### **Model 2: Human-Centric Work**

Focus on things AI can't do well: human connection, creativity, wisdom.

- **Who it's for**: People who value direct human impact
- **How it works**: Double down on uniquely human skills
- **Trade-off**: Smaller scale, harder to measure, less certain economics

### **Model 3: Portfolio Life**

Diversify across multiple domains: some AI-leverage work, some human-centric work, some non-economic pursuits.

- **Who it's for**: People who want balance and multiple sources of meaning
- **How it works**: Build a varied life with different value streams
- **Trade-off**: Complexity, less focus, harder to optimize

---

## The Money Question

<aside>Money matters, but it's not the only thing that matters. The AI age requires us to be more intentional about the money-meaning balance.</aside>

**Financial realities:**

- AI will create enormous wealth for some
- AI will disrupt many careers and industries
- Economic inequality may increase dramatically
- New opportunities will emerge unpredictably

**Strategic considerations:**

- Build skills that complement AI (taste, judgment, synthesis)
- Develop multiple income streams where possible
- Stay adaptable and willing to pivot
- Focus on scarce human capabilities

**But also:**

- Don't optimize purely for money
- Some of the most valuable work won't be well-paid
- Meaning and satisfaction matter for long-term happiness
- Time is your scarcest resource

---

## The Meaning Question

<tutorial>When work is no longer the primary source of meaning, we need to figure out what gives life purpose and value.</tutorial>

**Sources of meaning beyond work:**

- **Relationships**: Family, friends, community
- **Creation**: Art, craft, building things
- **Growth**: Learning, developing, becoming
- **Service**: Helping others, contributing
- **Experience**: Beauty, adventure, presence
- **Spirituality**: However you define it

**The challenge:**

We've made work the center of meaning for so long that many people don't know what else gives their life value. This is both problem and opportunity.

---

## Finding Your Path

<callout>There's no single right answer. The key is to be intentional about what you're optimizing for and why.</callout>

**Questions to ask yourself:**

1. What do I love doing that AI can't replace?
2. What kind of impact do I want to have?
3. How much money do I actually need?
4. What gives my life meaning beyond productivity?
5. What am I willing to trade off for what?

**Signs you're on the right track:**

- You're developing uniquely human capabilities
- Your work aligns with your values
- You have sources of meaning beyond economics
- You're excited about the future, not afraid of it
- You're building things that matter to you

---

## The Optimistic Take

<tutorial>The AI transition could be the beginning of a renaissance of human flourishing — if we navigate it wisely.</tutorial>

**What's possible:**

- More time for creative and meaningful work
- Less drudgery and routine cognitive labor
- Greater access to knowledge and tools
- New forms of art, science, and human expression
- Reconsideration of what makes a good life

**What's required:**

- Intentionality about what we value
- Courage to pursue non-obvious paths
- Community and support systems
- Economic policies that share the gains
- Cultural shift away from work-as-everything

---

## The Bottom Line

<callout>The AI age forces us to confront questions we've avoided for too long: What actually matters? What gives life meaning? What's worth doing with our limited time?</callout>

Money isn't going away, and neither is work. But the relationship between them is changing.

The people who thrive will be the ones who:

- Develop skills that complement AI, not compete with it
- Find meaning beyond economic productivity
- Build diverse sources of value and satisfaction
- Stay adaptable and open to new possibilities
- Live intentionally rather than by default

The future isn't about AI vs. humans — it's about humans figuring out what matters when AI handles so much of what used to.
