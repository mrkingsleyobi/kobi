<callout>We're not heading toward AGI — we're heading toward something weirder: a world where intelligence becomes a utility, and the question isn't "can machines think?" but "what happens when thinking becomes cheap?"</callout>

Everyone's obsessed with when AGI will arrive. They're asking the wrong question. The next four years won't be about *if* machines achieve human-level intelligence — they'll be about what happens when **intelligence becomes democratized, commodified, and embedded into everything**.

Here are my three predictions for 2026-2030, and why I think we're dramatically underestimating the convergence heading our way.

---

## 1. The Economic Unbundling: Half of All "Knowledge Work" Becomes a $50/Month Subscription

<aside>This isn't about job loss — it's about job transformation. The question is: will you be the one paying $50/month or the one collecting it?</aside>

By 2028, we'll see the economic unbundling of knowledge work that the internet did to media — just compressed into 24 months instead of 20 years.

**What this looks like:**

- **Software development**: AI pair programmers that handle 80% of implementation. Humans become architects and reviewers.
- **Legal services**: Contract review, document generation, and basic legal research become near-instant and essentially free.
- **Financial analysis**: Automated research reports, portfolio optimization, and risk assessment available to everyone.
- **Medical diagnosis**: AI diagnostic tools that outperform specialists in many domains, with humans as最后的sanity check.

**The economic shift:**

<definition>
  <term>Cognitive CRUD</term>
  <description>The routine cognitive work — Create, Read, Update, Delete of information — that makes up most knowledge jobs. This is what AI is about to commoditize.</description>
  <usage>"Most of our job is just cognitive CRUD. That's what AI will automate first."</usage>
  <cite>Coined in response to watching AI systems replicate entry-level work</cite>
</definition>

The jobs that survive will be:
- **High-stakes decision making** (where failure is catastrophic)
- **Complex physical coordination** (plumbers, surgeons, electricians)
- **Human connection at scale** (leadership, therapy, sales)
- **Novel synthesis** (connecting ideas AI hasn't seen before)

**Timeline:** This shift accelerates dramatically in 2026-2027 as enterprise AI deployments mature. By 2028, the economic disruption will be impossible to ignore.

<tutorial>The smartest career move? Develop skills that AI can't easily replicate: taste, judgment, and the ability to synthesize across domains. Become the person who knows what to ask the AI, not the person the AI replaces.</tutorial>

## 2. The Technical Stack Collapse: AI-Native Systems Replace Traditional Software

The software stack we've spent 40 years building is about to be disrupted from the top down.

**Here's what's coming:**

### **LLM-to-LLM Communication** (2026-2027)
AI systems will begin communicating directly with other AI systems in specialized protocols humans can't easily read. Negotiation bots haggling with supplier bots. Code generation systems talking directly to deployment systems.

<aside>We won't be able to read the logs. We'll just see outcomes. Most of the time, that'll be fine. Sometimes, it won't.</aside>

### **Self-Healing Systems** (2027-2028)
Software that debugs, patches, and optimizes itself. The DevOps engineer of 2028 is essentially a shepherd of autonomous software systems.

### **Reality Synthesis** (2028-2029)
AI systems that can generate convincing video, audio, and text in real-time. The distinction between "real" and "AI-generated" collapses. This is where things get philosophically weird.

**The security implications:**

Traditional cybersecurity assumes:
- Attackers are humans with human limitations
- Defenders can understand attack patterns
- Systems have bounded behavior surfaces

AI-native systems break all three assumptions:
- Attackers can be AI with AI-scale capabilities
- Attack patterns evolve faster than humans can analyze
- System behavior becomes fundamentally unpredictable

<callout>The cybersecurity challenge of 2028 won't be "stop hackers" — it'll be "prove your system is doing what you think it's doing."</callout>

## 3. The AGI Red Herring: We Get Something More Interesting Than AGI

Everyone's obsessed with AGI — artificial general intelligence that matches human capability across all domains.

But I think we're getting something weirder.

**My prediction:** By 2030, we'll have AI systems that are:

- **Superhuman at narrow tasks** (already here)
- **Competent at broad tasks** (coming fast)
- **Fundamentally alien in how they think** (the interesting part)

This isn't AGI — it's **ASI** (Artificial *Specialized* Intelligence) at scale.

**Why this matters:**

1. **We're building intelligence we don't understand**
   - These systems won't think like humans
   - They'll find solutions we can't comprehend
   - We'll be in the position of using tools we don't fully understand

2. **The "alignment problem" gets harder**
   - If we don't understand how they think, how do we align their values?
   - Current approaches rely on human supervision
   - Future systems may be beyond effective supervision

3. **The control paradox**
   - More capable AI = harder to control
   - More embedded AI = harder to turn off
   - More useful AI = harder to verify

<tutorial>The real question isn't "when AGI?" — it's "at what point does AI capability outpace our ability to understand, control, or verify it?" I think we cross that threshold before 2030.</tutorial>

## The Convergence No One's Talking About

Here's what keeps me up at night:

These three trends converge around 2028-2029:

1. **Economic disruption** from AI automating knowledge work
2. **Technical disruption** from AI-native systems we can't fully understand
3. **Strategic disruption** from AI capabilities outpacing our governance

<callout>We're not ready for any one of these. We're definitely not ready for all three at once.</callout>

The optimistic take: We get abundance, intelligence augmentation, and solutions to problems we can't currently solve.

The pessimistic take: We get economic dislocation, opaque systems making critical decisions, and a governance crisis.

The reality: Probably both, with massive variance between winners and losers.

**What I'm confident about:**

- **The direction is clear**: Intelligence becomes cheaper and more ubiquitous
- **The disruption is real**: Traditional knowledge work faces massive pressure
- **The philosophical questions matter**: We're not ready for what we're building

## What To Do About It

<aside>You can't stop these trends. You can position yourself to benefit from them rather than being disrupted by them.</aside>

**Practical steps:**

1. **Develop AI literacy**
   - Understand what these systems can and can't do
   - Learn to work with AI as a collaborator
   - Stay current on capabilities

2. **Focus on human-centric skills**
   - Taste and judgment
   - Complex communication
   - Synthesis across domains
   - Leadership and influence

3. **Build anti-fragile systems**
   - Diversify your skills and income
   - Build things that AI can't easily replicate
   - Create value through relationships and trust

4. **Think about the long term**
   - What problems become more valuable when intelligence is cheap?
   - What human needs persist regardless of technology?
   - Where can you create unique value?
